# Polymer pilot acceptance plan

The public repository includes a synthetic Polymer Geospatial demonstration so anyone can reproduce the workflow. No real entity structure, product specification, customer, agreement, license, payment or valuation has been asserted. The real pilot belongs in a separate private workspace.

## Start with operating facts

Establish the founders and their residence/working locations; any existing entity, commitments or IP; the first buyer and paid problem; the intended delivery and data sources; available capital; responsibilities and review authority; the actual relevant jurisdictions; and whether acquisition is an active option or only a comparison.

The system should surface missing facts, not manufacture a confident business plan. Record the initial assumptions and the decisions they affect. Select the applicable jurisdiction guide only after a qualified review of the operating footprint; retain the decision and its basis. The jurisdiction change records the reason and reviewed evidence, keeps earlier guide steps as superseded history and reopens completed work.

## Smallest useful operating cycle

1. Name an actual prospective buyer and record a source-backed problem.
2. Define a paid offer with scope, exclusions, acceptance, timing and unit economics.
3. Review founder/IP arrangements, data/software rights, actual legal/tax requirements and provider setup.
4. Model the downside cash need, including labor, source costs, support and collection delay.
5. Prepare and obtain review of the agreement and delivery process; execute through authorized people and providers.
6. Deliver a usable result, preserve customer acceptance, issue an invoice externally and reconcile the receipt.
7. Compare actual effort, costs, revenue and collection timing with the forecast in a recorded decision.
8. Assign continuing customer, license, renewal, reporting and financial-close responsibilities.

A completed synthetic example proves software behavior. A first paid, accepted and repeatable real delivery tests business usefulness. Entity formation, a signed contract, revenue and collected cash are separate outcomes.

## If acquisition becomes a live route

Keep the same business records. Add a target-specific thesis, verified source financials, owner-replacement costs, customer concentration, rights/consents, liabilities, normalized earnings, financing and a funded transition. Compare the acquisition with building, licensing or partnering. Review an explicit walk-away price and downside. After closing, track the transfer of access and responsibilities and compare actual results with the purchase thesis.

The v0.1 screening model does not implement a full quality-of-earnings engagement, legal diligence, purchase accounting, multiple debt tranches, seller earnouts or equity IRR. Extend and independently review those calculations before relying on them for a real transaction.

## Acceptance evidence

| Question | Evidence of usefulness |
|---|---|
| Can another person understand the business? | A reviewed working pack with visible unknowns and original sources |
| Can the team repeat delivery? | Named responsibilities, source rights, an acceptance standard and a second observed delivery |
| Does work complete honestly? | Provider receipts and human assessments distinct from prepared drafts |
| Are the economics credible? | Reconciled actual effort, costs, invoice and bank receipt compared with a saved forecast |
| Do corrections propagate? | A changed assumption or withdrawn source reopens dependent decisions and marks prior documents stale; a jurisdiction change also marks earlier deal analyses and forecasts |
| Can the data be recovered? | A successful restore and integrity check on a separate private database |
| Can public users reproduce the software? | Fresh installation, synthetic demonstration, automated checks and usable documentation |

Record the reviewer's ordinary corrections and observed workflow outcomes. Do not make “looks good” scores a substitute for whether the business work actually completed.

## Public release boundary

Publish software, schemas, generic work guides, synthetic examples, methodology and carefully anonymized lessons. Keep original commercial data, identities, source licenses, executed documents, financials and private reviews outside Git. Refine shared components when the private pilot exposes a defect; publish the fix and a synthetic regression case, not the underlying private record.
