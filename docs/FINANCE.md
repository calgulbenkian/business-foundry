# Financial methodology

All models are deterministic and use Python Decimal arithmetic. Outputs are decimal strings rounded half-even to cents for money and four decimal places for ratios. A value that rounds to zero is shown as `0.00` or `0.0000`, never with a minus sign. Inputs are assumptions unless supported by reviewed evidence; the models are not audited accounts, licensed market comparables, a fairness opinion or individualized investment advice.

## Inputs and precision

Every decimal input to the forecast and the acquisition screen carries at most 12 decimal places: the money, unit, rate, growth and multiple fields, reported EBITDA and each earnings adjustment amount. Places are counted on the value, so trailing zeros do not count, and a zero has none however it is written: `2000.500000000000000` is accepted because it equals `2000.5`, and `0E+999999999999999999` and `-0E-999999999999999999` are accepted as zero and shown as `0.00`. A value with more places, such as `0.0000000000001` (1E-13), is refused with a validation error that names the field; it is not rounded. The upper and lower bounds of each field are unchanged.

Send decimal inputs as JSON strings, such as `"0.2"` and `"2000.50"`. The interface does this, and the example files in `examples/` use strings. A JSON number, in an API request, a tool call or a command-line configuration file, is read as a binary floating-point value before it becomes a Decimal, so it keeps at most 17 significant digits: `1234567890.123456789` is stored as `1234567890.1234567`, without an error. A number whose floating-point form has more than 12 decimal places, such as the `0.30000000000000004` that `0.1 + 0.2` produces or the `1000.3000000000001` that `1000.1 + 0.2` produces, is refused by the 12-place limit, in a rate or an amount alike.

Every figure of the acquisition screen is exact for accepted inputs: it is the exact value of its formula, rounded half-even once, to cents for money and to four decimal places for the coverage ratio. This covers normalized EBITDA, both ends of the enterprise-value and equity-value ranges, every line of sources and uses (buyer cash at closing and funding beyond uses among them), the year-1 and the amortizing annual debt service, each yearly seller note payment and the balloon, and each scenario's EBITDA, cash available for debt service, cash after debt service, coverage ratio and amortizing coverage ratio. Sums, differences and products are formed in precisions sized to their operands, with inexact results trapped, so no intermediate value is rounded. Each loan's level payment is formed as an exact fraction and the loans' payments are added exactly. A deferred seller note's balloon, principal × (1 + rate)^years, is an exact decimal. Cash after debt service and the coverage ratio are each formed as one exact fraction over that total and rounded once, with enough digits for the figure's own size and places; a rounded debt service is never used in a calculation. The cash-shortfall flag is set exactly when the exact cash after debt service is below zero. The forecast is still calculated at 40 significant digits before its results are rounded for display.

With these limits every input is bounded in size and in decimal places, so the exact acquisition steps are bounded in digits too. The most significant digits an accepted input can give each step are:

| Step | Digits |
|---|---:|
| Normalized EBITDA, total uses, buyer equity required, buyer cash at closing | 25 |
| Scenario EBITDA (the upside adds 5% of revenue, two places below the revenue's last) | 27 |
| Debt interest, and EBITDA less that interest | 37 |
| Enterprise value and equity value | 37 |
| Tax | 48 |
| Cash available for debt service | 49 |
| A seller note payment (a deferred 30-year balloon, below 10^22 with 372 places) | 394 |
| Debt-service denominator, three 30-year loans | 14,164 |
| Debt-service numerator, three 30-year loans | 14,200 |
| Cash available, and cash after debt service, over the debt-service denominator | 14,212 |

The year-1 and the amortizing debt service each have their own fraction. With a seller note that amortizes they are the same. With one that is interest only or deferred, the year-1 fraction has only the senior and property loans' denominator, so the amortizing one, with all three loans, is the larger, and the counts above are its counts.

The bounds tests reach each of these counts with accepted inputs and check that no tested deal exceeds them. They also run every input at its smallest positive value (1E-12) and at its largest accepted value in both models, and check that every non-zero value calculated at those corners lies between 1E-100 and 1E+46, apart from the exact fractions over the debt service (the payment's numerator and denominator, and cash available and cash after debt service scaled by that denominator), which are calculated without a limit on size. If a model still cannot evaluate an input, the save is refused with an explanation and nothing is stored; the same idempotency key can then be used with corrected inputs.

Plans and deals saved before the 12-place limit keep their inputs and results. They can still be read, exported and used in documents, and the audit still passes; submitting those inputs again is refused until they are written with at most 12 decimal places.

Model versions:

- Forecast 1.1.1 limits every decimal input to 12 decimal places, shows zero without a minus sign whatever its exponent, and formats values of any size. Otherwise it calculates as 1.1.0.
- Acquisition 1.1.0 adds a separate real estate price, a property loan, annual rent, seller note schedules and investor equity, described under Acquisition screening below, and the figures they need: buyer cash at closing, funding beyond uses, the yearly seller note payments and balloon, the amortizing annual debt service and the amortizing coverage ratio. A request that gives none of the new inputs gives every figure that 1.0.1 gave, unchanged: each new input then takes an example value that adds nothing to any sum and no payment to the debt service, so 1.1.0 calculates the 1.0.1 figures by the same steps. The annual debt service of 1.1.0 is what is due in year 1; for a deal whose seller note amortizes, as every 1.0.1 note did, that is the 1.0.1 figure.
- Acquisition 1.0.1 limits every decimal input to 12 decimal places, shows zero without a minus sign whatever its exponent, and calculates every figure exactly, as described above. Version 1.0.0 calculated at Python's default 28 significant digits and divided by a debt service that was itself calculated at 28 digits. For the same inputs a 1.0.1 figure can therefore differ from 1.0.0 wherever 1.0.0 rounded a value before its final rounding to the other side of a rounding boundary: annual debt service; cash after debt service, the coverage ratio and the cash-shortfall flag, which depend on it; cash available for debt service when the debt interest, the tax or a step of the sum that forms it needs more than 28 significant digits (up to 49); and the enterprise-value and equity-value ranges when normalized EBITDA × multiple, or its sum with cash acquired and debt assumed, needs more than 28 significant digits (up to 37). For inputs that 1.0.1 accepts, normalized EBITDA, scenario EBITDA and the sources and uses need at most 27 significant digits, so they equal 1.0.0, apart from the sign of zero.

Saved plans and deals keep the version and results they were calculated with. A deal saved under 1.0.1 has none of the 1.1.0 figures; its page and investment memo show the figures it has, as before, and nothing stored is rewritten.

A model version changes when the figures a model gives change or new ones appear, as in 1.1.0. It does not change when only the requests a model accepts change, as below.

The inputs 1.1.0 added are left out of the request an idempotency key guards when the caller does not give them. So a request that gives none of them has the fingerprint it had under 1.0.1: a key used before replays the stored result, and the same key with any new input, even at its example value, is a different request and is refused with HTTP 409. Left out, each always takes the same example value, so nothing that decides the figures is missing from the fingerprint.

Requiring the three figures below and recording `assumed_inputs` did not change acquisition 1.0.1. A model version names how the figures are calculated, and every input that is accepted gives the same figures as before. The change is in which requests are accepted: a request without one of the three figures is now refused, where earlier it was calculated with an example value.

Making the example senior debt zero also left acquisition 1.0.1 unchanged, by the same rule. The calculation is the same, and a deal's stored inputs hold every value the model used, so the same inputs give the same figures. What changed is an example value: a request that leaves out `senior_debt` is now screened with no senior loan, where earlier it was screened with a loan of 400,000 at 9% over 7 years. Deals saved earlier keep their stored inputs and results, and their inputs show the loan they used.

## Operating model

The forecast supports 1–60 months. A month is 30 days. Model version 1.1.1 calculates active units and fixed costs in month `t` (1-indexed) as:

- `new_units_t = monthly_units × volume_factor × (1 + monthly_growth)^(t−1)`
- `units_t = new_units_t + monthly_retention × units_{t−1}`, with `units_0 = 0`
- `fixed_t = (fixed_costs + (fixed_cost_step if t ≥ fixed_cost_step_month else 0)) × fixed_factor`

Revenue is units × price. Cost of sales is units × unit cost; fixed costs are paid in their month. With the default `monthly_retention = 0` and no fixed-cost step, the results equal version 1.0.0, apart from the sign of zero.

`monthly_retention` (0–1) carries that share of the prior month's active units into the next month, an aggregate recurring-unit carryover. It is not a per-cohort ledger and has no contract terms, tiers or aging. `fixed_cost_step` is an extra monthly fixed cost (for example a hire or a lease) from `fixed_cost_step_month` onward. A positive step requires a start month within the forecast horizon, and a start month requires a positive step. The assumptions should include wages, benefits, software, support, insurance and other operating expenses appropriate to the business.

Receipts lag billed revenue by the selected collection delay, including a fractional-month interpolation. For a 45-day delay, half of a month's sales is collected the following month and half in the month after that. Revenue less receipts accumulates in receivables; initial receivables are zero. Costs do not wait for customer payment.

Opening cash equals contributed equity plus opening debt. Interest accrues on opening monthly debt. Principal repayments stop at zero outstanding debt. Constant monthly capex is depreciated straight-line over the selected asset life, starting in the purchase month. Tax is a planning rate on positive EBIT less interest, paid in the same month; no tax refund is invented for losses.

The income statement calculates revenue, cost of sales, fixed costs, EBITDA, depreciation, interest, tax and net income. The cash flow statement starts from actual modeled receipts and cash expenses, then deducts capex and debt principal. The balance sheet includes cash, receivables, net fixed assets, outstanding debt and equity plus retained income. The unrounded identity `assets − liabilities − equity = 0` is checked each month. Individually displayed rounded lines can differ by a cent. Yearly columns in the business plan add up the displayed monthly amounts, so a yearly total can differ by cents from an unrounded total such as total revenue in the summary.

Negative cash explicitly means an unfunded requirement. It remains visible rather than creating hidden borrowing. Additional cash needed is the absolute value of the lowest negative balance. It is an estimate under the chosen assumptions, not a committed financing amount. The first shortfall month is decided on unrounded cash, so a shortfall of less than half a cent sets it while the minimum cash and additional cash needed show `0.00`.

| Scenario | Units relative to base | Fixed costs relative to base |
|---|---:|---:|
| Base | 100% | 100% |
| Downside | 75% | 110% |
| Upside | 125% | 105% |

The unit factor multiplies new units. Because the retention recurrence is linear, it scales retained units and total units in every month by the same factor. The retention rate itself is the same in every scenario. The fixed-cost factor applies to both base fixed costs and the scheduled step.

These stresses are not probability estimates. Create additional model versions to explore price, churn or retention, sales timing, funding, collection delays or other assumptions. There is no per-cohort recurring-revenue engine, inventory, accounts payable, multi-currency consolidation, sales/VAT tax engine, tax-loss carryforward, deferred tax, complex financing or statutory reporting in v0.1. The tax-rate input is not a jurisdictional tax recommendation. A forecast saved while a different planning jurisdiction was in force is marked stale, with a reason naming the jurisdiction it was saved under and the one in force now, and the business plan prints that warning above its figures. The mark blocks no step. Check the forecast's assumptions, the tax rate among them, and save a new version; a saved forecast is never recalculated or rewritten.

## Discounted cash flow

Unlevered free cash flow equals EBIT less modeled tax on positive EBIT, plus depreciation, less capex and the change in receivables. Each monthly flow is discounted at the selected effective annual rate with a fractional-year exponent.

The terminal cash-flow base is the last 12 months of unlevered free cash flow, multiplied by `1 + terminal growth`. Terminal value is that amount divided by `discount rate − terminal growth`, discounted to the present. The discount rate must exceed terminal growth. As the two approach each other, the terminal value grows without limit; the smallest accepted spread is 0.000000000001. A narrow spread is visible as a terminal share close to 1. Fewer than 12 months, or a nonpositive terminal cash-flow base, suppresses DCF output.

Enterprise value is the sum of the discounted operating and terminal values. Equity value adds the separate excess-cash input and subtracts opening debt. Cash the forecast itself accumulates is not added. Do not treat operating cash already needed by the business as excess cash. Terminal share is displayed because it reveals how much of the result depends on an assumption beyond the forecast horizon. High growth in the final forecast year can materially distort the terminal base; normalize a new scenario before relying on it. A scenario value is not evidence of a buyer's price.

## Acquisition screening

An analysis needs the three figures that describe the target and its price: annual revenue (`annual_revenue`), reported EBITDA (`reported_ebitda`) and the negotiated purchase consideration (`purchase_price`). They have no example value. A request without one of them is refused with a validation error that names each missing field, and nothing is saved: no deal, no diligence step and no use of the idempotency key.

Every other figure has an example value, such as transaction fees of 30,000 or a senior interest rate of 9%. When a request leaves a figure out, the model uses that value, and the deal records the field in `assumed_inputs`, in the field order of the inputs. It lists exactly the figures not given that the model used. A figure that is sent is never listed, even when it equals the example value. Adjustments and evidence are lists of what the caller claims, not figures with an example value, so they are never listed. The stored inputs hold every value the model used. The deal page and the investment memo name each assumed input with its value, under **Inputs not given**. The API, the command line and the MCP tool record the same list for the same request. A deal saved before this list existed has no `assumed_inputs`. It is shown as not recorded, never as having no assumed inputs, and nothing stored is rewritten. A retry with the same idempotency key and the same request returns the same deal and list. A request with the same key that leaves out different figures is a different request, even when every value is the same, so it is refused with HTTP 409.

Financing is the buyer's choice, not a fact about the target, so the example senior debt (`senior_debt`), property loan (`property_debt`), seller note (`seller_note`) and investor equity (`investor_equity`) are zero: a deal given no financing is screened as bought without debt, with the buyer's own cash. The example real estate price (`real_estate_price`) and rent (`annual_rent`) are zero too. A loan's rate and term, and the seller note's schedule, enter the arithmetic only when the loan is above zero. So `assumed_inputs` lists a rate, term or schedule that was not given only when its loan is above zero; with no loan they are not used and not listed. A deal with no loan says on its page and in the investment memo that no debt was given and none was assumed, so its empty debt service and coverage read as intended, not missing.

The inputs are refused when the high multiple is below the low multiple, when debt funding (senior debt, property loan and seller note) exceeds transaction uses, and when reported EBITDA exceeds revenue. Investor equity beyond what the uses need is not refused; it is shown as funding beyond uses. When a check counted a figure the request did not give, the refusal names that figure and its example value and asks for it, for example: "High multiple must not be below low multiple. The high multiple was not given, so its example value 5 was used; give a high multiple." The debt check names only figures whose example value is not zero, since a zero adds nothing to either side. A refusal that counted only given figures keeps its plain words. The API, the command line, the MCP tool and the web form give the same message.

Normalized EBITDA starts with reported EBITDA, subtracts the entered replacement-management cost, includes only adjustments linked to human-reviewed evidence, and subtracts the annual rent:

- `normalized = reported_ebitda − replacement_management + Σ reviewed adjustments − annual_rent`

`annual_rent` is the occupancy cost the business will pay after closing when it does not own its building, such as when the seller keeps the building and leases it to the buyer. Enter only rent the reported EBITDA does not already bear: a business that already rents its premises has that rent in its EBITDA. Like replacement management, rent is a cost of the business, so it lowers the earnings the multiples value and is deducted before debt service in every scenario, the downside and the upside included. Unsupported seller add-backs remain visible and excluded. Supported adjustments can be positive or negative. Review does not make a source independently audited, and normalization does not verify the reported starting EBITDA.

The enterprise-value range is normalized EBITDA × the user-entered low/high multiples. No market multiple is fetched or inferred. Nonpositive earnings suppress the range. The valuation equity bridge adds the entered cash and subtracts entered debt; this bridge is distinct from transaction funding.

`purchase_price` is the price of the business. A building sold with it is priced separately in `real_estate_price`. The multiples value the business only, so compare the value range with the business price, not with the total. The real estate is shown at its entered price, beside the value range, and the model does not value it: no appraisal, rent yield or capitalization rate is calculated.

Transaction uses and sources:

- `total_uses = purchase_price + real_estate_price + fees + working_capital + integration_cost`
- `buyer_equity_required = total_uses − senior_debt − property_debt − seller_note` (equity after all debt, from the buyer and any investors)
- `buyer cash at closing = buyer_equity_required − investor_equity`

When the buyer's cash at closing is above zero, the page and the memo say how much the buyer puts in. When it is zero, they say plainly that the buyer puts in no cash. When it is below zero, `buyer_cash_at_closing` is `0.00` and `funding_beyond_uses` shows the difference: debt and investor equity bring in more than the deal uses, and the founder must decide where the extra goes. Investor equity is a source of funds only: investor returns, preferences and ownership are not modeled. Debt funding cannot exceed uses. Enter a cash-equivalent consideration consistent with the chosen asset/share transaction. Existing debt repayment, retained liabilities, acquired cash availability, working-capital pegs and closing adjustments require explicit treatment in these inputs and the diligence record. The `debt_assumed` field is only a valuation bridge; it does not add financing or debt service. A deal retaining separate target debt needs an extended debt schedule before commitment.

Senior debt and the property loan each use level monthly amortization, annualized: for principal `P`, annual rate `r` and term `n` years, the annual payment is `12 × P × m / (1 − (1 + m)^−12n)` with `m = r / 12`. Zero-interest loans divide principal evenly over the term. A lender may amortize a building over a longer term than a business (often up to 25 years against 10); the property loan's example rate is 8% and its example term 25 years, used only when a property loan is given.

The seller note follows `seller_schedule`, over `seller_years` years. The end of the term is the balloon:

- `amortizing` (the example): the level payment above in every year, and nothing left at the end.
- `interest_only`: `P × r` in each year, and in year `n` the principal as well, `P × r + P`. The balloon is `P`, with no accrued interest, since the interest is paid each year.
- `deferred`: nothing until year `n`, when `P × (1 + r)^n` is due. Interest accrues once a year on the balance, the interest of earlier years included (annual compounding). The balloon is `P × (1 + r)^n`, of which `P` was lent and `P × ((1 + r)^n − 1)` is accrued interest. For example 350,000 at 6% over 5 years: 350,000 × 1.06^5 = 468,378.95, which is 350,000 lent plus 118,378.95 of interest. Simple interest, which this model does not use, would give 455,000.

The output lists the seller's payment for every year of the note's term (`seller_note_schedule.payments`) and, for an interest-only or deferred note, the balloon: its year, amount, principal and accrued interest. A one-year interest-only or deferred note is due whole at the end of year 1.

The model screens one year, year 1. Annual debt service is what is due in year 1: the senior and property loans' level payments plus the seller note's year-1 payment under its schedule, added exactly and rounded once to the cent. Cash after debt service, the coverage ratio and the shortfall flag use it, so a deferred note adds nothing to them and an interest-only note adds only its interest. A balloon in a later year is outside the screened year; the memo says so, and the founder must plan how it will be paid. Beside it, the **amortizing** coverage (`amortizing_coverage_ratio`, over `amortizing_annual_debt_service`) counts the seller note as if it amortized over its term. Lenders often test a deal that way, and the two together tell the founder whether a bank would count the deferral. For an amortizing note the two are the same, and the page and memo show one.

The base tax approximation uses initial annual interest on every loan, `senior_debt × debt_rate + property_debt × property_rate + seller_note × seller_rate`, whether that interest is paid or, on a deferred note, accrued, and excludes depreciation. Whether accrued interest is deductible is a tax question for the jurisdiction. Cash available for debt service is normalized earnings less that tax, maintenance capex and working-capital growth. The displayed coverage ratio divides this cash by the exact year-1 debt service; it is not necessarily the definition used in a lender covenant. With no debt service in year 1, the ratio is null rather than infinite. The cash-shortfall flag is decided on the unrounded amount after debt service, so a shortfall of less than half a cent sets the flag while the amount shows `0.00`.

The downside subtracts 10% of revenue from EBITDA with no cost relief. The upside adds 5% of revenue. These explicit illustrative stresses exclude automatic synergies, debt repricing, purchase accounting, earnouts, exit proceeds, the refinancing of a balloon and equity IRR. A source change marks the saved deal stale and blocks approval of its diligence steps; save and review a new analysis to use the changed evidence. A deal saved while a different planning jurisdiction was in force is also marked stale, with a reason naming the jurisdiction it was saved under and the one in force now. That mark blocks no step. Check the analysis's assumptions, the tax rate among them, and save a revised analysis before relying on its figures. A saved analysis is never recalculated or rewritten.

### Lender rules

Foundry applies no lender or government loan rule: it does not decide whether a deal qualifies for a loan, how much equity a lender requires, or whether a seller note counts toward it. When a deal has a senior or property loan, the investment memo names the U.S. Small Business Administration's rules only as something to check. SBA 7(a) loans are governed by SBA Standard Operating Procedure 50 10, "Lender and Development Company Loan Programs" (https://www.sba.gov/document/sop-50-10-lender-development-company-loan-programs). As checked on 13 November 2026 its current version is 8.1, effective 1 October 2026. Its predecessor, 50 10 8 (effective 1 June 2025), required a buyer's equity injection of at least 10% of project costs for a complete change of ownership, and counted a seller note toward that injection only when the note is on full standby (no principal or interest payments) for the life of the SBA loan and covers no more than half of the required injection. An interest-only or deferred seller note in this model is not a full-standby note unless its terms say so. These rules change; check the version in force with the lender before relying on any structure.

## Validation

The tests compare the first operating cycle with independent hand calculations (including unit retention and fixed-cost step cases in every scenario), check 0/15/30/45/60/90-day receipt timing and revenue conservation, trace interest/principal/depreciation through all statements, and verify loss treatment, funding gaps, asset lives and DCF. Acquisition tests check supported versus unsupported adjustments, amortization, zero debt, sources equaling uses and invalid input combinations. For 1.1.0 they check, against hand-worked figures, a business and its building priced separately with two loans, a business bought without its building paying rent, a seller note on each schedule with its yearly payments and balloon (compounded annually, not simply), investor equity that leaves the buyer some cash, none, and more funding than uses, the amortizing coverage beside the year-1 one, and that a request giving none of the new inputs gives main's figures and main's idempotency fingerprint, written into the test from main's own output. They also check that a request without revenue, reported EBITDA or the purchase price is refused and stores nothing, that `assumed_inputs` names exactly the figures a request left out and the model used, a loan's rate and term only when the loan is above zero, that a deal given no financing is screened without debt, and that a refusal caused by an example value names it. Bounds tests compare each payment and the total of the loans with exact rational arithmetic across the accepted rates, principals and terms, and compare every acquisition figure with an independent rational calculation, for random deals and for deals built to sit on a rounding boundary, or within 1E-20 either side of one, of cash available, cash after debt service, the shortfall flag, the coverage ratio and the value ranges. They check the 12-place limit for every decimal input of both models, the digit counts above, and run every input at its smallest positive and largest accepted value. No live company financials are used in the test fixtures.
